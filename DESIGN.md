## Overview

Next GM should feel like a premium wrestling GM game cockpit: a night-time production room where the player is a booker, TV producer, talent evaluator, and executive under pressure. The design is dark, sharp, tactical, and broadcast-driven. It borrows from sports draft shows, wrestling TV graphics, command-center monitors, and console franchise menus.

The system must never collapse into a generic SaaS dashboard. When the interface shows numbers, they should read like a game HUD or scouting desk, not an admin KPI board. When the interface shows cards, they should feel like broadcast panels, save posters, profile frames, draft boards, lower-thirds, and monitor plates.

## Colors

The base palette is nearly black with high-contrast cool text. The visual life comes from controlled brand light rather than bright surfaces.

- **Background:** deep black-charcoal, used as the arena room and app canvas.
- **Surfaces:** layered blue-black plates that remain dark enough to feel backstage, but separated through borders, inner highlights, and scanline texture.
- **Text:** off-white for primary labels, cool silver for secondary copy, and muted slate for tertiary metadata.
- **Brand accents:** red, electric blue, gold, and fight-card gold variants may skin the same layout. They should affect hero lighting, active navigation, selected states, left rails, borders, and major actions without turning the whole screen into a solid color page.
- **Status colors:** warning, danger, success, and info are compact signal colors for badges, rails, meters, and alerts. Status meaning must never rely on color alone.

## Typography

Typography is role-driven.

- **Display:** heavy, condensed, uppercase, and broadcast-like. Use it for the game title, major screen names, brand names, command strip headlines, and dramatic labels such as draft-night status.
- **UI body:** readable and practical. Use it for normal panel copy, compact explanations, buttons, and control labels.
- **Data:** monospaced or technical. Use it for ratings, dates, budgets, pick numbers, percentages, status IDs, metadata rows, and meter labels.
- **Ticker:** narrow, urgent, and short-form. Use it for dirt-sheet fragments, alert summaries, lower-thirds, IWC pulse, and pressure labels.

Do not use decorative fonts for dense data. Display text may be dramatic, but body and data text must stay readable on dark glass.

## Layout

The layout is viewport-first and laptop-first. Major screens should fit inside the visible app viewport; the page itself should not become a scrolling website. If a screen needs more content, put overflow inside contained panels, tabs, drawers, modal-style details, or scrollable lists.

The shell uses a compact top HUD and a bottom-centered glass dock when the player is inside the game shell. On title and setup screens, the flow should be guided by screen actions rather than global navigation. The dock is icon-first by default and may expand on hover or focus, but it must overlay the screen rather than push content around.

Composition should vary by mode:

- Title and campaign entry screens use one large cinematic identity area plus a focused action stack.
- Save selection uses poster-like save cards and recovery/empty states.
- Setup screens feel like a contract office, launch packet, and war-room preparation flow.
- Draft screens become denser, with an ON THE CLOCK command panel, scouting feed, selected talent spotlight, board preview, and lower-third notes.
- Brand dashboard uses one dominant hero command strip plus a few connected game hubs, not equal-weight analytics cards.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth comes from tactical glass, tonal layering, borders, under-glow, and monitor-like inset highlights. Shadows should be broad and dark, while glow should be brand-colored and purposeful.

Use glass as a serious broadcast material, not soft frosted decoration. Panels should have 1px edge definition, subtle inner highlights, scanline or LED texture, and restrained brand-lit under-glow. Avoid muddy black-on-black by preserving clear borders and text contrast.

## Shapes

The shape language is sharp sports broadcast geometry. Corners are intentionally tight: 2px controls, 3px panels, and only slightly softer dock geometry. Do not use bubbly cards, pill overload, circular decoration, rounded SaaS tiles, or soft blob backgrounds.

Angled cuts, left rails, top bars, lower-thirds, and hard monitor frames are appropriate when they improve hierarchy. Use them consistently and sparingly so the interface feels produced, not cluttered.

## Components

Primary actions are cinematic in hero areas and compact in command surfaces. Disabled actions must look intentionally locked, not broken. Secondary buttons are dark glass or outline treatments, not pale neutral SaaS buttons.

Badges and labels should be compact broadcast tags. Use short text such as Preview Build, Draft Controls Locked, Scouting Feed, Board Preview, Brand HQ, Week 1 Setup, Urgent, Important, Optional, and Blocked.

Cards are not generic containers. Use save posters, GM archetype cards, wrestler portrait frames, scouting rows, command monitors, alert strips, title panels, rivalry heat cards, and dashboard mode tiles. Management-heavy cards prioritize scan speed; character-facing cards need identity, portrait space, or silhouette framing.

Meters should feel like compact production readouts. Use hard rectangular tracks and brand fills. Never expose hidden formulas as precise truth when the product should communicate uncertainty.

## Do's and Don'ts

- Do make the interface feel like a premium wrestling broadcast command center.
- Do keep screens viewport-first with contained scrolling.
- Do use brand accents for lighting, selection, rails, and CTAs.
- Do preserve strong contrast on dark surfaces.
- Do use portrait or poster frames where wrestling identity matters.
- Do make the next player action obvious within two seconds.
- Do keep labels short, player-facing, and game-like.
- Do provide non-color cues for critical statuses.
- Do respect reduced motion by removing transform-heavy hover motion.
- Don't make full-page scrolling the default interaction model.
- Don't build equal-card walls or corporate KPI rows.
- Don't use bright SaaS panel fills, soft blobs, random decorative accents, or oversized rounded cards.
- Don't put budget, fans, alerts, or long status copy inside the dock.
- Don't make brand skins feel like separate apps.
- Don't use official logos or image-dependent identity unless approved assets exist.
- Don't expose raw backend, diagnostic, service, or implementation wording in player-facing UI.
